Soldier Paths · add a soldier

Add someone from your family

If someone in your family served in the Second World War, you can add him to the map. You don't need to know anything about maps or data. Your own AI assistant (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or similar) reads your documents and writes them up in the form this site needs. You check the result here, then send it in.

1 Gather what you have

Anything helps. The most useful, roughly in order:

  • His service file from Library and Archives Canada, or his discharge certificate
  • Newspaper clippings, obituaries, letters home
  • Photos, especially with writing on the back. Give them to your AI too, so it can caption them and say which part of his story each belongs to.
  • What the family remembers: his unit, where he went, stories he told, anyone he met

Photos of pages are fine. Leave out anything about people who are still living.

2 Give your AI this prompt and your documents

Copy the prompt, start a new chat with your AI, paste it in, then attach or paste your documents. Add anything you know from memory in your own words.

3 Paste the AI's answer here

Paste its whole reply. This page checks it and shows you what would go on the map. Nothing is sent yet.

4 Send it in

Submissions are read by a person before anything goes on the site. Each fact keeps a note of where it came from, and anything from family memory is marked as such.

The easiest way to send it is to upload it:

  1. Tick both boxes above.
  2. Press Upload the file and photos. Your file is saved to your computer or phone, and an upload page opens in a new tab.
  3. On the upload page, add the file you just saved and your photos. Use the original photo files, with the same names your AI listed.
  4. Type your name and your code (it starts with SP-, shown below) so we can match everything up, then press Upload.

You don't need an account. Uploads go to a private folder that only the site's owner can see, and nothing goes on the site until a person has read it.

Other ways: download the file and send it with your photos to whoever pointed you to this site, or use "Send through GitHub" (public, and needs a free GitHub account).
